# The evaluation gap — what the 2026 memory-system results do and do not say about AWM

Written 2026-10-11. Builds on `landscape-2026-10-10.md` §3–§5 rather than restating it: that
document established *what* three independent 2026 results found. This one answers the next
question — **whether those findings transfer to AWM, which parts of AWM they cannot see, and what
AWM would have to measure to stop the question being rhetorical.**

Three new measurements were run for this document, all against the frozen eval snapshot, all
reproducible, none costing API spend. They changed the conclusion twice and refuted a claim an
earlier draft of this document made. The scripts are in the session scratchpad.

**Provenance labeling, applied per claim.** *Verified* = a query, probe or grep was run and the
output read. *Observed* = read in a paper's or a file's own text. *Assumed* = inference. Every
number names the store it came from, because there are three stores in play and they do not agree:

| store | engrams | active | fading | archived | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tests/realstore-eval/snapshot/store.db` (frozen 2026-08-24) | 29,853 | 11,337 | 392 | 18,123 | **every published figure, and every probe here** |
| `~/.awm/memory.db` (live, read at the start of this session) | 20,697 | 7,773 | 430 | 12,493 | what the agent actually uses |
| `~/.awm/backups/memory-2026-08-25-00-39-48.db` | 29,871 | 11,342 | 392 | 18,136 | the only source of pre-fade bodies |

*Verified by SQL against all three.* **The live row drifts and is context only** — it grew by 13 engrams while this document was being written, from this session's own writes; every figure in this document is measured on the frozen snapshot. The live store is smaller than the snapshot because of the 2026-10-10 store-split recovery, not because of pruning. **Consequence that governs this whole
document: no new measurement may be run on the live store and compared to a published figure.**

## 1. The gap, stated precisely

There are three non-overlaps, not one. Only the first was named in the landscape scan.

**1. The regime non-overlap** (already argued): all three external results measure 50–475
synthetic sessions, one-shot seeded, where the history fits in context. AWM's corpus is 11,337
active engrams of real accretion, 1,234,429 tokens to carry whole. *Observed / verified.*

**2. The dependent-variable non-overlap:** the papers measure **answer correctness**. AWM's
headline measures **whether the gold engram ranked first**. 93.0% and 78.4% are not the same
quantity, so AWM has no number comparable to theirs in *either* direction — it cannot be beaten by
them and it cannot claim to beat them. *Verified against `docs/benchmarks-current.md`.*

**3. The population non-overlap — the finding that matters most.** AWM's benchmark fixtures select
**against** the exact mechanisms the papers condemn. Measured against the frozen snapshot:

| fixture | probes | golds with faded bodies | golds archived | median gold body |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fixture.json` (identifier) | 1,316 | **0** | **0** | 2,077 chars |
| `fixture-category.json` (topic) | 450 | **0** | **0** | 2,011 chars |
| `fixture-temporal.json` | 101 | **0** | **0** | 1,872 chars |

*Verified.* The snapshot contains 392 faded engrams and 18,123 archived ones — **60.7% of it is
archived**. **Not one of those engrams is a gold, across all 1,867 probes.**

So AWM's benchmark has the same blind spot as the papers', for the opposite reason. They never
tested long accretion. AWM never tests its own lifecycle. Everything AWM publishes is true of the
engrams the lifecycle left alone.

## 3. Why AWM may perform *worse* than their analysis implies

The regime caveat protects AWM from their *datasets*. It does not protect it from their
*mechanisms*, and §3.1 is now a measured instance of the exact failure 2605.08538 describes.

### 3.1 Fade retains 7.1% of answer-bearing identifiers, and prefix truncation cannot fix that

**The experiment.** 38 engrams are faded in the live store and still full in the frozen snapshot —
all 38 of 38 present, verified. That makes a **paired** design possible on the reproducible
snapshot: same engram, full body versus faded body. Mean body **1,505 → 159 chars, 89.4% of the
text destroyed**; 29 of the 38 had `access_count ≥ 3`.

Three arms over those 38 engrams, k = 10, clock pinned to the snapshot, two query types each —
the engram's own **concept** verbatim (does the cue survive), and a **deep** query built from a
12-word window drawn from *past char 150*, the region fade deletes (can it still answer):

| arm | concept @1 | concept @10 | deep @1 | deep @10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. full body, full embedding** (snapshot as shipped) | 27/38 | **37/38** | 37/38 | **37/38** |
| **2. faded body, stale embedding** (what ships today) | 30/38 | **37/38** | 2/38 | **9/38** |
| **3. faded body, re-embedded on the truncated text** | 30/38 | **37/38** | 3/38 | **10/38** |

*Verified, this session.* **Read this table only as a test of the re-embedding fork, which is what
it was built for.** The deep query is a verbatim window from the deleted region, so BM25 cannot
match it after truncation and the 37 → 9 fall is close to guaranteed by the probe's own design. It
is not a measure of answerability, and an earlier draft of this document labeled it as one. What
the table does establish:

1. **Re-embedding is neutral — neither the fix nor a risk.** 37/38 → 37/38 on findability, 9 → 10
   on deep queries. An earlier draft claimed re-embedding would make most faded engrams
   unfindable; **that claim was wrong and is withdrawn.** The reason is in
   `src/core/retrieval-text.ts:73`: `buildRetrievalText` is `concept + content` plus topical tag
   terms, so re-embedding on the truncated body **keeps the concept and the tags in the vector**.
   *Verified.* One limit on this arm: both query types are *verbatim* text, and the vector
   channel's distinct contribution is paraphrase matching, which was not tested. The
   recommendation does not change, but the result is narrower than "re-embedding costs nothing."
2. **Findability is unchanged within noise.** Concept @10 is 37/38 in every arm; concept @1 moves
   27 → 30, which is 3 of 38 and not worth interpreting. Fade does not make an engram harder to
   find — and that is the problem, because 9 of 38 deep queries still rank an engram whose answer
   is gone, and `success@1` cannot see it.

**The answerability question needs a different instrument, and the fixture already holds it.**
`fixture.json` carries `offset` and `contentLen` for all 1,316 identifiers, where `offset` is
measured in `concept + ' ' + content` space. An identifier survives a prefix truncation at `KEEP`
if it sits inside the concept, or if `offset < len(concept) + 1 + KEEP`. That needs no retrieval at
all. **Method check: this computation reproduces the fixture's own `beyondTruncation` flag exactly
— 1,039 of 1,316, 79.0%, matching the figure `runner.ts`'s docblock states independently.**
*Verified.*

| `KEEP` | answer-bearing identifiers retained |
|---|---|
| **150 — shipped** | **93/1,316 · 7.1%** |
| 300 | 244/1,316 · 18.5% |
| 400 | 348/1,316 · 26.4% |
| 500 | 439/1,316 · 33.4% |
| 750 | 647/1,316 · 49.2% |
| 1,000 | 783/1,316 · 59.5% |
| 1,500 | 999/1,316 · 75.9% |
| 2,000 | 1,119/1,316 · 85.0% |

*Verified.* Only 18 of 1,316 identifiers (1.4%) sit inside the concept and therefore survive any
truncation. **At the shipped constant, fade destroys 92.9% of the identifiers a future query would
ask by.**

**And this is the finding that changes the recommendation: prefix truncation is the wrong *shape*,
not 150 the wrong *constant*.** Reaching 75% retention needs `KEEP ≈ 1,500` against a median gold
body of 2,077 chars — which is not fading. There is no prefix length that both saves meaningful
space and keeps the answers, because identifiers are distributed through the body rather than
concentrated at its head: 79.0% of them sit beyond char 400. Sweeping `KEEP` would only locate the
probe's own window. See §8.1 for what to do instead.

For the record on how fade selects: `FADE_PROTECTED_ACCESS_COUNT = 10`
(`src/engine/consolidation.ts:112`, enforced at `:549`), and fade only ever considers engrams
accessed at least once, so the population it truncates is **engrams with 1–9 demonstrated
recalls**. Fade calls `updateContent` and `updateStage` at `:570-571` and never `updateEmbedding`,
which is deliberate: `src/storage/sqlite.ts:851-853` keeps `fading` in the vector channel on
purpose — *"faded engrams retain their embedding … so they should still participate in vector
recall."* Arm 3 shows that rationale is not load-bearing for verbatim queries. *Verified.* Note
also that 1,305 of the 1,316 golds (99.2%) exceed `FADE_MIN_CONTENT_LEN = 250`, so essentially the
entire benchmark population is fade-eligible — it has simply not aged into it. *Verified.*

This revises the previous session's framing, which held fade's behavior was unmeasured and that the
paper supported the guard already shipped. The guard stands. "Unmeasured" was the wrong word.

### 3.2 Archive looks like the adaptive-budget cliff — but the evidence for that is confounded, and the mechanism is not what it appears

60.7% of the frozen snapshot and ~60% of the live store sit in `archived`. In the live store,
**649 archived engrams had `access_count ≥ 3`** before archiving; on the `work` agent alone, 1,631
archived rows carry some prior access history. *Verified.* That is the shape of a problem. But two
probes show the obvious reading does not hold up.

**First probe — reachability by stage.** 25 `work` engrams per stage, each queried with its own
concept verbatim at k = 10:

| stage | n | found @1 | found @10 | in BM25 candidate pool | mean `access_count` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fading` | 25 | 19 | **21** | 25/25 | 4.6 |
| `active` | 25 | 8 | **9** | 9/25 | 6.2 |
| `archived` | 25 | 2 | **2** | 4/25 | **0.3** |

*Verified, this session.* **Two confounds, both disqualifying for the simple reading, and the
second one matters more:**

- **Body length.** The `archived` sample's median body is 137 chars and the `active` sample's is
  138 — so these two are the matched pair, and the honest contrast is **9/25 against 2/25**, not
  21/25 against 2/25. An earlier draft dismissed the `active` arm as unrepresentative while
  keeping `archived`; the same reasoning discards or keeps both.
- **Access history.** Mean `access_count` is 0.3 for the archived sample against 6.2 for active
  and 4.6 for fading. **The archived engrams in this sample are mostly the never-used ones.** Their
  being unretrievable may be the lifecycle working correctly, not a failure. ACT-R activation
  differs across the arms, so stage is not isolated.

**Second probe — where they drop out.** `searchBM25WithRankMultiAgent`
(`src/storage/sqlite.ts:683-710`) carries **no stage predicate at all** — only `retracted = 0` —
so an archived engram is nominally visible to the lexical channel. Asking that function directly:
only **4 of 25** archived engrams appear in the BM25 candidate pool for their own concept, and 2
reach the final top 10. For `active`, 9 enter and 9 survive. *Verified.*

So the binding constraint is **first-stage candidate generation, not a stage filter and not
downstream re-ranking** — BM25 takes an inner top-50 across the whole FTS index, and these engrams
lose that competition. That matches the documented miss-stage split, where 5 of 10 benchmark misses
"never entered the candidate pool." It also means the two-tier retrieval idea in §8 is aimed at a
filter that does not exist for the lexical channel, and is demoted accordingly.

**What is still true and still unmeasured:** AWM's 60.7% store reduction has never been placed on
the spectrum between 2605.08538's +21.8 pp corpus result and its −32.4 pp budget result, and the
649 archived engrams with 3+ prior recalls are not explained by the never-used sample above. A
matched measurement — same access history, same body length, stage the only variable — is work
that has not been done. §7, arm A4.

### 3.3 The mechanism that would invert the result is idle

"Expect the gap to invert" rests on learning from repeated use. AWM's Hebbian strengthening is
validation-gated on `memory_feedback`, which agents call about **once per 170 recalls**, and a
120-query probe found the association graph contributes a score to 85% of top-3 results while
changing the top-3 set in **0%** of them — a boost capped at 0.25 never overturns the
cross-encoder. *Observed, `docs/known-limitations.md`.*

AWM therefore cannot claim the inversion today, even inside its own regime. The machinery that
would produce it is wired but inert.

### 3.4 No stage-transition timestamp exists, so lifecycle quality is not computable post-hoc

The `engrams` table carries `created_at`, `last_accessed` and `retracted_at`. There is no column
recording when `stage` changed. *Verified by reading all 34 columns.* Fade regret and archive
regret cannot be computed from the live store at all. This is the instrumentation prerequisite for
the one metric on which this class of system has a published win.

### 3.5 Two further channels measure at or below baseline

Temporal: every cue phrasing scores *below* the 94.1% no-cue baseline on the frozen snapshot; the
oracle week-filter ceiling is 96.0%, 1.9 pp up. *Observed, `benchmarks-current.md`.* Graph: §3.3.
Two of the pipeline's distinctive channels are currently decorative.

## 4. Why AWM may perform *better* than their analysis implies

**Structural, holds now.** *(a)* Naive in-context learning is not an available baseline —
1,234,429 tokens cannot be carried, so CL-Bench's winning arm does not exist here. The right
competitor is not "full history in context" but **Claude Code's auto memory, which loads the first
200 lines or 25 KB of `MEMORY.md` and drops the rest.** That is the arm to beat, and it is a
curated-index arm, not a stateless one. *(b)* merge-on-reinforce is dedup-with-accumulation — the
single mechanism that did not trail baseline in 2605.08538.

**Plausible, untested.** *(c)* ACT-R decay over real age spread means activation actually varies;
2605.08538 ran every experiment at uniform A = 1.0 and reported maturation as untested. AWM has the
signal their study could not generate. *(d)* Repeated, related queries over months is the setting
the paper's authors named as the one they expect to invert the result. AWM is the only system in
the scan with such a corpus — but see §3.3 for why it cannot yet exploit it. *Assumed for (c) and
(d): architectural arguments, not measurements.*

One narrower favorable finding from §3.1 worth keeping: fade's stated design goal — that a faded
engram still surfaces by cue — **is working**, at 37/38. The problem is what happens after it
surfaces.

## 5. Co-worker challenge — what was taken and what was rejected

`/ask-coworker` (gpt-5.3-codex) was given the external results, AWM's architecture and numbers,
and both lists above, and asked to attack the regime caveat rather than vote on it. Prompt and
reply are in the session scratchpad (`codex-gap-prompt.txt`, `codex-gap-reply.md`).

**One premise in the prompt was false, and it propagated.** The prompt stated that archive makes
engrams unreachable because retrieval filters `stage='active'`. §3.2 shows the lexical channel has
no stage predicate. Codex's "archive at least is explicit removal" framing and its two-tier
proposal both rest on that false premise, so both are discounted below.

### Taken

1. **The caveat half-survives, and the surviving half is narrower than claimed.** Codex: survives
   — "AWM has real long-horizon accretion and non-fit context; papers under-test that exact
   operational setting." Does not survive — "therefore their conclusions don't apply," because
   "the mechanistic failure signatures are already present in your live store." Taken in full, and
   §3.1 then supplied the measurement that proves it.
2. **Fade is the more damaging of the two**, because "archive at least is explicit removal; fade is
   deceptive retention." Taken, and the measurement is stronger than the argument: 37/38 → 9/38
   answerability at unchanged findability.
3. **The arm set is five, not twelve.** Taken as §7, including the arm this document would not
   have proposed on its own: **A4, lifecycle suppression off**, which is now the only way to
   resolve §3.2's confound.
4. **The paired fade study is a mechanism test, not a population estimate.** Taken, including the
   label: *paired degradation assay*. It was then run (§3.1) and reported under exactly that
   label — n = 38, no claim about prevalence or time-to-regret.
5. **"Support sufficiency is where fade damage appears."** Taken as the right diagnosis, with a
   correction below on the premise.
6. **A lifecycle harm budget is a target in its own right.** Taken as T4.
7. **The closing verdict**, quoted because it is worth arguing with: *"right now AWM looks like
   good lexical retrieval + expensive reranker + destructive lifecycle policy. Prove incremental
   value or simplify."* A0 and A2 settle the first two clauses. §3.1 settles the third against AWM.

### Rejected or corrected

1. **"Support sufficiency is a new axis you don't measure" — factually wrong, and the correction
   cuts against AWM.** AWM already publishes it: *"of the golds retrieved, 99.7% actually contain
   the answer"*, and `tests/realstore-eval/runner.ts:228-260` credits token economics **only** to
   sufficient recalls, with a comment explicitly rejecting the earlier version for treating a
   ranked-but-unhelpful result as a win. Codex did not know because the prompt did not say.
   *Verified.* But **that 99.7% is measured on 300 golds of which 0 are faded and 0 are archived**,
   median body 2,077 chars (§1). The metric exists, is the right metric, and is pointed away from
   the failure. The fix is a **fixture change, not a harness change** — stratify the existing
   sufficiency metric by lifecycle stage and body length. Cheapest high-value item here.
2. **"Re-embed on fade" — measured, and it is neutral.** Codex proposed recomputing the embedding
   from the truncated text so a faded engram stops ranking on a body that no longer exists. Arm 3
   of §3.1 tested it: findability unchanged at 37/38, deep queries 9 → 10. It neither helps nor
   harms, so it is not the fix. An earlier draft of this document rejected it on the grounds that
   it would destroy findability; **that draft was wrong, and the measurement is the reason this
   section says so.** Limit worth stating: both query types in that arm are verbatim text, and the
   vector channel's distinct job is paraphrase matching, which was not tested.
   **Codex's framing was also better than this document's first use of it.** Its deep-query
   suggestion was built to test the fork and does test it; relabeling that same arm as
   "answerability" was this document's error, corrected in §3.1 by a measurement that needs no
   retrieval at all.
3. **Two-tier retrieval over the archive filter — demoted to an open question.** It presumes a
   stage filter in the retrieval path. There is none in BM25 (`sqlite.ts:683-710`), and §3.2 found
   the binding constraint is first-stage candidate generation. The idea may still be right for the
   *vector* channel, which does exclude `archived` by construction, but the proposal as framed
   targets the wrong stage.
4. **Latency target p50 ≤ 400 ms — rejected as stated.** It contradicts T1. p50 is 474–500 ms with
   ~83% of it cross-encoder, and the cross-encoder is what bought +7.4 pp when tags entered the
   rerank passage. A 400 ms p50 means cutting the component carrying the measured gain. Replaced
   with a no-regression bound (T5).
5. **"Drop the graph from the hot path" — taken; "remove the graph" — rejected.** The graph is
   retained for the state it accumulates — supersede chains, causal links, co-recall history — and
   `known-limitations.md` already says so. Removing it from scoring is defensible; deleting it
   loses the supersede chain `memory_supersede` depends on.
6. **Human-adjudicated answer correctness on 200 probes — deferred, not rejected.** The only
   proposal requiring sustained human labeling, which is the one resource a solo maintainer cannot
   scale. Scoped to 100 probes and placed behind A0–A4, which are automatable and which decide
   whether the adjudication is worth buying.

| # | metric | baseline | target | why this number |
|---|---|---|---|---|

It is also the only form in which the field will read an AWM number |
| **T2** | Identifier retention under fade: share of `fixture.json`'s 1,316 answer-bearing identifiers that survive the fade policy | **measured: 7.1% at the shipped `FADE_KEEP_CHARS = 150`** (§3.1) | **≥ 75%, while retaining no more than 40% of the body** | The only target here with a measured baseline rather than a hoped-for one. Meeting both halves requires changing the *shape* of truncation, not the constant |
| **T3** | Sufficiency@3, **stratified by lifecycle stage and body length** | the unstratified 99.7% on `fixture.json` | **faded stratum ≥ 89.7%** — no worse than 10 pp below matched non-faded controls | Puts §3.1's finding into the suite that gates releases, rather than a one-off probe |

alongside sufficiency@3 stratified by stage (T3).** Also stop pairing "93.0%" with "29,853
engrams"; the searched population is 11,337 and the page already says 11,262 retrievable.

## 7. The arm set, and what not to build

Five arms, all retrieval-level, all against the frozen snapshot, no API spend. **None exists
today**: `tests/realstore-eval/runner.ts` reads only `REALSTORE_SNAPSHOT`, `FIXTURE`, `K`,
`GRANULARITY`, `TRACE`, `LIMIT`, `OFFSET`, `NO_SHUFFLE` and `NOW`, and calls
`activation.activate()` directly. There are no channel-ablation switches anywhere in the harness.
*Verified by grep.* **This corrects the landscape scan's §4**, which treated the missing stateless
baseline as blocked on Memory Gauntlet probe determinism: only the end-to-end arm is blocked. The
retrieval-level arms are not blocked — they are unwritten. The three probes in this document are
evidence that arms of this kind run on this machine in minutes.

Ranked by information per unit effort:

1. **A0 — BM25-only (FTS5).** Answers MemoryAgentBench on its own terms and supplies T1's
   denominator. Must-have; nothing else on this list means anything without it.
2. **A4 — lifecycle suppression off, on a matched sample.** Include archived engrams in the
   candidate pool, hold access history and body length constant across arms. This is now the only
   way to resolve §3.2's confound, and it supplies T5.
3. **A2 — hybrid, no cross-encoder.** Is the reranker the whole story? 83% of recall latency says
   this is the arm that could simplify the product.
4. **A1 — dense-only.** Does the vector channel add anything over lexical, or only cost.
5. **A3 — full pipeline.** The reference arm. It is today's published number; it only becomes
   meaningful next to A0.

Plus one that is not an ablation: the **25 KB curated-index arm** — Claude Code auto memory's
actual budget, since that is the competitor a user chooses between, not "full history in context."

**Not worth building now.** Query-expansion on/off and Rocchio on/off (low ROI until A0–A4 land);
graph on/off (already measured at 0% top-3 change); abstention-threshold variants (orthogonal until
retrieval utility is established).

**Operational constraint, which is also a correctness fix:** `scripts/bench-current.mjs` refuses to
start below `BENCH_MIN_HEADROOM_GB` (default 8) because five eval runs have been OOM-killed on this
machine. Precompute the candidate pool once and replay it across arms rather than running the
pipeline five times — that also removes the only source of between-arm variance, which is the exact
failure that parked the Gauntlet. Assert identical pool membership between arms before comparing
any score.

**On reproducing §3.1 at larger n:** 38 is the ceiling, not a sampling choice. All 38 engrams faded
in the live store are full in the snapshot, 38 of 38; the snapshot's own 392 faded rows are faded
in the August backup too, 392 of 392, so no original survives for them. *Verified.*

## 8. System changes, ranked

Measurement does not improve a system. These do, and none needs an LLM at write or maintenance
time.

1. **Change the shape of truncation, not the constant — and surface the `[faded]` marker.** §3.1
   measured the shipped policy at **7.1% identifier retention**, and measured that no prefix length
   reaches a useful target without effectively disabling fade: 75% needs `KEEP ≈ 1,500` against a
   2,077-char median body, because 79.0% of identifiers sit beyond char 400. Re-embedding changes
   nothing (arm 3). So the lever is neither the embedding nor the number — it is that a **prefix**
   is the wrong thing to keep.
   What to keep instead: the head *plus the spans around answer-bearing identifiers*. This is
   achievable without an LLM, since the fixture builder already locates identifiers
   deterministically by token shape — but it is **new code, not reuse**: the existing
   `entity_mentions` table holds ~12,550 tag-derived entities (`proj:`, `version:`) across ~3,920
   engrams with **no offsets**, so it cannot serve as a span index as it stands. *Verified.* Scope
   the extractor before costing the change.
   Independently, and cheaply, do this first: **the recall payload should say the body is
   partial.** The engram currently ranks first and answers nothing, and nothing tells the agent to
   re-derive the fact. One field converts a silent failure into a visible one, and it is correct
   whatever is decided about truncation's shape.
2. **Record a stage-transition timestamp.** One additive column, one write at each `updateStage`
   call site (`src/engine/consolidation.ts` lines 571, 625, 648, 652, 719, 795). Without it, T4 is
   uncomputable and every lifecycle decision is unauditable. Cheapest item in this document, and it
   gates the most valuable target.
3. **Widen or tier first-stage candidate generation — not the stage filter.** §3.2 found the
   binding constraint is BM25's inner top-50 across the whole index, not a stage predicate: 4 of 25
   archived engrams and 9 of 25 active ones even entered the pool for their own concept. That is
   also where the documented benchmark misses die (5 of 10 "never entered the candidate pool"). A
   two-tier fallback into `archived` may still be right for the **vector** channel, which does
   exclude it by construction (`sqlite.ts:845-856`), but the first-stage pool is the larger and
   better-evidenced target. **Measure A4 before building either.**

Demoted: **drop the graph from the scoring path** but keep the structure. It changes no result
today (0% of top-3 sets) and costs a traversal on every recall. Keep the edges for supersede chains
and causal links.

## 9. Five-perspective production-failure debate

Each perspective names a trigger, a failure and an impact. The subject is this document's own
recommendations.

**Requirements.** Trigger: A0 is built and reports BM25-only within 1 pp of the full pipeline on
identifier probes. Failure: T1 fails, and the honest reading is that four stages of machinery are
decorative on the workload AWM is actually asked to serve. Impact: the product's positioning
inverts, and `docs/claims.md` becomes wrong in public. This is the *intended* risk of measuring —
but it should be planned for before the number exists.

**Architecture.** Trigger: span-preserving fade ships with a deterministic identifier extractor
written for the purpose. Failure: that extractor's recall differs from the fixture builder's, so the
retention curve in §3.1 does not describe the shipped policy. This project has already paid for
exactly that drift once: in the public-corpus generator, a generator and its consumer disagreed by
about 93 chars on "the same" offset, and the discrepancy looked like a tuning failure until the two
definitions were forced to agree. Impact: a fade policy that reports 75% retention and delivers
something else. Mitigation: the fade extractor must **call** the fixture builder's identifier
definition rather than reimplement it, and assert zero mismatch against the 1,316 known offsets.

**User (the agent).** Trigger: the `[faded]` marker is surfaced in the payload but no guidance
accompanies it. Failure: the agent treats a 150-char stub as the whole fact, which is exactly
today's behavior with the marker now visible and ignored. Impact: unchanged silent failure, plus a
false sense that it was addressed. Mitigation: the marker must come with the instruction to
re-derive, in the recall output itself, not in the docs — the same reasoning as CLAUDE.md's
volatility rubric, which exists because a confidently wrong fact costs more than no fact.

**QA.** Trigger: arms are run across five separate pipeline executions on a machine that has
OOM-killed five eval runs. Failure: an arm dies mid-run, or between-arm variance from
nondeterministic pool construction is mistaken for an effect — the failure that parked the Gauntlet.
Impact: a wrong conclusion that is expensive to detect, in the same shape as the three benchmark
errors already documented this month. Mitigation: one precomputed pool replayed across arms, with
identical pool membership asserted before any comparison.

**Product.** Trigger: T4 is published as retention precision alongside 2605.08538's 97.2%. Failure:
the definitions differ — theirs is measured on a labeled corpus with known relevance, AWM's would be
measured on *subsequent recall*, which is endogenous, since an engram recall cannot reach is an
engram that will not be recalled. Impact: a headline that looks comparable and is not, the specific
failure mode this project has already corrected three times. Mitigation: read their definition
before implementing; if it cannot be matched, publish AWM's under a different name.

**Unresolved after the debate:** the *cost* side of span-preserving fade — §3.1 measures what prefix
truncation retains but not what a span policy would cost in store size, which is what decides
whether it is worth building; and whether §8.3's effort belongs on the first-stage pool or on the
vector channel's archive exclusion, which needs A4.

## 10. Verdict

The regime caveat is **half right, and the half that survives is narrower than the landscape scan
claimed.** What survives: the three results do not measure AWM's corpus, naive ICL is not an
available baseline here, and maturation under real decay is untested by anyone. What does not
survive: the inference that their conclusions therefore do not apply. Their central mechanical
warning — destroying retrievable specifics costs more than it saves — is now **measured on AWM at
7.1% identifier retention under the shipped fade policy**, with findability untouched, which is the
most unfavorable possible shape: the metric AWM publishes is precisely the one fade does not
degrade.

Two findings are worth carrying out of this document on their own. First, **AWM's benchmark gold
population and AWM's lifecycle output population do not intersect** — 0 faded and 0 archived golds
across 1,867 probes. Second, **three of this document's own earlier conclusions were wrong and were
corrected by measurement**: re-embedding on fade is neutral rather than harmful; archived engrams
are lost to first-stage candidate generation rather than to a stage filter; and the fade problem is
the *shape* of prefix truncation rather than the value of its constant — a conclusion the first
instrument, a verbatim deep query, could not have reached, because it could not survive truncation
by construction. Every one of those errors came from reading code and reasoning, and every one took
a single probe to settle. That is the method this project already claims, applied to itself.

Nothing here reopens fade as a design, and the guard shipped on 2026-10-10 stands. What it reopens
is whether a prefix is the right thing to keep — and the answer, measured, is no.

**Verdict: PASS on the analysis, BLOCK on the build.** Every recommendation in §6–§8 is a build or
a measurement that needs Robert's sequencing call against the four items already scheduled in
`decision-gap-closure-2026-10-10.md` (M1 → S1 → S2 → S3 → S4), because A0 and §8.2 both compete
with M1 for the same measurement slot. The task priorities filed alongside this document are
provisional for that reason. `/full-audit` does not apply: the deliverable is markdown with no
lint, security or UI surface.
